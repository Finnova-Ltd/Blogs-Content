APRA 3% Serviceability Buffer Explained: How Banks Calculate Your Borrowing Power

Understand how the APRA 3.0% assessment buffer, Household Expenditure Measure (HEM), and debt-to-income caps determine your maximum borrowing capacity.

📅 19-Aug-2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ R BAKSHI (Principal Broker)

Understand how the APRA 3.0% assessment buffer, Household Expenditure Measure (HEM), and debt-to-income caps determine your maximum borrowing capacity.

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) mandates that authorized deposit-taking institutions (ADIs) assess whether prospective borrowers can afford repayments at an interest rate at least 3.00 percentage points higher than the actual loan rate.

If a bank offers a variable rate of 5.95%, your income and expenses will be stress-tested at 8.95% across a 30-year principal and interest term.

Lenders calculate living expenses using the greater of your declared living costs or the Melbourne Institute’s Household Expenditure Measure (HEM). Declaring unrealistically low expenses will not inflate borrowing power, as the automated HEM floor is applied automatically.

💡 What Affects Your HEM Assessment?

  • Number of Dependants: Each child reduces borrowing capacity by approximately $40,000 to $65,000.
  • Single vs Joint Incomes: Dual-income households benefit from lower combined living expense assumptions.
  • Private School Fees & Discretionary Debts: Ongoing non-essential commitments must be declared and factored in.

Banks assess credit cards based on the total credit limit, not the balance owed, assuming a monthly repayment of 3% to 3.8% of the entire limit.

A dormant credit card with a $15,000 limit reduces borrowing power by roughly $60,000 to $70,000. Reducing limits or closing unused facilities prior to application unlocks immediate capacity.

Under APRA exceptions, several major and second-tier lenders offer a 1.0% modified serviceability buffer for borrowers refinancing dollar-for-dollar without cash out, enabling homeowners in mortgage prison to secure lower rates.
