NDIS & SDA Property Investment Finance in Australia: The Complete Lending Guide

Specialist Disability Accommodation (SDA) property investment under the NDIS offers projected 8%–14% gross rental yields. Learn how to secure non-bank, construction, and SMSF finance from specialist Australian lenders.

Specialist Disability Accommodation (SDA) property investment under the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) has become one of the most discussed wealth-creation strategies in Australia. Offering projected gross rental yields between 8% and 14% and stable, government-backed funding packages, SDA homes offer both strong financial returns and crucial, life-changing housing for Australians with significant disabilities.

⚡ Specialist Lending Notice: Traditional major retail banks frequently decline NDIS construction and investment loans due to specialized valuation methods, non-standard lease structures, and stringent building compliance codes. At EZ Mortgage Broker, we connect you directly with specialist non-bank and commercial lenders across Victoria, New South Wales, and Australia-wide.

Specialist Disability Accommodation refers to purpose-built, accessible housing tailored for NDIS participants who require specialized housing due to extreme functional impairment or very high support needs. When applying for an NDIS property loan, standard bank assessment calculators do not apply. Lenders focus on three critical underwriting criteria to evaluate serviceability and security value:

💰CRITERION A

Cash Flow & Income Shading

While an NDIS property may generate $80,000 to $120,000+ gross annual income, lenders shade rental income to standard market residential rent during the initial build and enrollment phase.

✓ Verifies personal income buffer during vacancy
📐CRITERION B

Valuation (Alternate Use Value)

Because SDA homes have specialized modifications, lenders value the home on an Alternate Use Value (what the property is worth if sold as a standard residential home to the open market).

✓ Valuation Gap: Covered by investor equity
📊CRITERION C

LVR Limits & SMSF Rules

Standard residential NDIS purchases qualify for up to 70%–80% LVR through specialist non-bank lenders. SMSF purchases qualify for up to 60%–70% LVR under an LRBA structure.

✓ 70%–80% Residential / 60%–70% SMSF

Self-Managed Super Funds (SMSFs) are a popular vehicle used by Australian investors to acquire SDA property due to substantial tax concessions. Super fund borrowing rules (LRBA) require a Single Part Contract structure or specialized funder solutions, minimum liquidity balances of $350k+, and strict adherence to arm's-length sole purpose test regulations. In accumulation phase, earnings are taxed at 15%, dropping to 0% in retirement pension phase.

Financing the construction of an SDA home involves specific progress drawdown stages verified by bank quantity surveyors: Deposit & Base Stage (15%–20%), Frame Stage (20%), Lock-Up Stage (25%), Fixing Stage (20%), and Practical Completion & SDA Enrolment (15%–20%). Once final compliance certification is issued by an accredited SDA Assessor, the dwelling is formally enrolled with the NDIA.

Which lenders in Australia finance NDIS and SDA properties?

Major retail banks generally do not provide direct retail mortgages for private SDA builds. Financing is primarily provided by specialized non-bank lenders (e.g. La Trobe, Bluestone, RedZed, Firstmac), boutique credit unions, and commercial mortgage managers.

Is rental income guaranteed by the government?

No. While the SDA funding attached to the participant is government-funded through the NDIS, income depends on the property being enrolled and occupied by an eligible tenant. Investors must manage potential vacancy periods.

Can I refinance my existing residential equity to fund an NDIS deposit?

Yes. Many investors release equity from their owner-occupied home or standard investment portfolio to fund the required 20% to 30% deposit and closing costs.
